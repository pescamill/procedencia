Cotton

Its original name:

  • ichcatl (Nahuatl) [1]

The cotton species used to make almost all the clothing on the planet was domesticated in the Yucatán Peninsula.

Verdict

Gossypium hirsutum, the cotton that clothes almost the whole planet, was domesticated in the Yucatán Peninsula region. [2] · Supported

What the sources show

  • The genomics of hundreds of accessions places the domestication of G. hirsutum in the Yucatán Peninsula, with later gene flow between forms. [2]
  • It is about 90% of the world's cotton; Egyptian and Pima cotton are another species, G. barbadense, from South America. [3]
  • In Europe, cotton was an imported good: its cultivation spread from India through the Islamic world between the years 700 and 1000, and Italian weavers depended on raw cotton shipped from Syria, Armenia and Cyprus. India was the world's largest maker of cotton cloth. [4, 5]
  • Mesoamerica, by contrast, wove its own cotton, from a species domesticated here. Every six months the province of Tochtepec alone delivered to Tenochtitlan 1,600 richly decorated mantles, 800 striped mantles and 400 huipiles with their skirts. [6, 7]
  • Cotton mantles (quachtli) served as money for large purchases; cacao, for small ones. [8, 9]
  • In July 1519, Cortés sent Charles V cotton mantles, white, chequered and coloured, along with undershirts, handkerchiefs, bedspreads, tapestries and carpets of cotton. According to López de Gómara, they were worth more for their workmanship than for their material. [9]
  • In 1780 the New Spain historian Francisco Javier Clavijero wrote that the Mexicans made broad cotton cloths as delicate and fine as hollands, the benchmark fine cloth, and highly esteemed in Europe. [9]
