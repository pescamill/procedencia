Food

Guava

Its original name:

  • xalxocotl (Nahuatl): gritty fruit [1]

The fragrant fruit of Christmas ponche. Mexico holds very diverse guavas, but the broadest genetic study places the origin of its domestication in Amazonia; that is why it is not claimed.

Verdict

Not claimed: the broadest genetic study, with 215 plants from 11 countries, places the origin of its domestication in Brazilian Amazonia, from where it reached Central America and Mexico. Mexico has its own, very diverse gene pool and named it xalxocotl, but the oldest evidence is not here. [2, 3] · Supported

What the sources show

  • There are more archaeological records of guava, and older ones, in South America than in Mesoamerica; in Mesoamerica historical records abound. [3]
  • A study of 215 plants from 11 countries found one gene pool in Mexico and four in South America; Mesoamerican populations have high genetic diversity. [2]
  • Dispersal models point to Brazilian Amazonia as the probable origin of domestication, from where it spread to the Andes, northern South America, Central America and Mexico. [2]
  • The WCVP, consulted via GBIF, records it as native only to Peru. It is a narrow record; that is why the genetic and archaeological studies weigh more here. [4]
