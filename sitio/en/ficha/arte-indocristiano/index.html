Indigenous sculpture and painting in churches

Its original name:

  • tequitqui (Nahuatl): tributary; the name Moreno Villa gave this art [1, 2]

The murals, reliefs and plasterwork that Indigenous artists made in colonial churches, with jaguar warriors and Tlaloc's paradises among the saints.

Verdict

The reliefs, murals and plasterwork of many colonial churches were made by Indigenous artists, who blended Christian iconography with forms and meanings from their own tradition. [2, 3, 4] · Supported

What the sources show

  • In the Augustinian convent of Ixmiquilpan, Hidalgo, begun in 1550, the murals show Indigenous warriors dressed as jaguars and coyotes, with pre-Hispanic weapons, among European acanthus leaves. [3]
  • The decoration of Santa María Tonantzintla, Puebla, blends the Assumption of the Virgin with images of Tlalocan, Tlaloc's paradise. [4]
  • Moreno Villa called the art of Indigenous people after the conquest tequitqui; the term is still used, though disputed. [2]
