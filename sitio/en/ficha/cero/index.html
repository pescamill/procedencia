Zero

Its original name:

  • mih (Classic Maya): nothing, zero [1]

The idea of «nothing» as a number. It was invented here independently, centuries before India, to write calendar dates.

Verdict

Zero was invented here, independently and centuries before India: the Long Count of the Mesoamerican calendar needed it to write its dates, and the oldest ones are in Chiapas and Veracruz. [2, 3, 4] · Supported

What the sources show

  • The oldest known Long Count date is on Stela 2 at Chiapa de Corzo, Chiapas: 7.16.3.2.13, the year 36 BCE. [2]
  • The first Long Count dates appear outside the Maya area, in the lands of the Olmec and their heirs, such as Tres Zapotes, Veracruz; that is why the use of zero is credited to them before the Maya. [3]
  • The Classic Maya gave zero signs of their own, such as the three-petaled flower or the shell, read mih, «nothing». [5, 1]
  • The zero written all over the world today comes from India's decimal system, firmly documented from the 7th century and brought to Europe through the Arab world. The Mesoamerican zero is an independent and earlier invention, not its ancestor. [4]
  • A study proposes that Mesoamerica could conceive of zero because it was used to writing the same number in several ways. [2]
