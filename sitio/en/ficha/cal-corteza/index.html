Construction

Lime plaster with bark sap

Its original name:

  • chukum (Yucatec Maya): tree whose bark is added to lime [1]

Lime mixed with sap from trees like the chukum. It makes plaster stronger and water-resistant; it is still used in Yucatán.

Verdict

Maya masons mixed lime with sap from the bark of trees such as the chukum. The mix makes plaster stronger and less soluble in water, and materials science studies it today to conserve buildings. [1, 2] · Supported

What the sources show

  • Mesoamerican masons have used bark extracts from the chukum, chacté, chacah and jabín trees to prepare lime plaster since pre-Hispanic times. [1]
  • Ancient plasters from Copán contain bark polysaccharides; replicas made with those extracts turned out tougher and more resistant to weathering. [2]
