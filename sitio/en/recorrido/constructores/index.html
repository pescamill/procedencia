Journey

Mexican builders

From the pyramids to the cathedrals, and from the cathedrals to the railroads of the United States: the peoples of this land raised buildings with their own techniques, and then built those of others. This journey follows those hands.

  1. Pyramidal platforms: Temples on mountains made by hand.
  2. Talud-tablero: A profile all of Mesoamerica copied.
  3. Lime plaster with bark sap: The lime that materials science studies today.
  4. Coatequitl: collective building labor: The rotating labor that raised everything else.
  5. The churches on top of the temples: The churches on top, and the nameless hands.
  6. Open chapel: A kind of church invented here.
  7. Indigenous sculpture and painting in churches: Jaguar warriors among the saints.
  8. Cornstalk sculpture: Cornstalk Christs that traveled to Spain.
  9. The braceros' withheld wages: The tracks and fields of the United States.

What the sources show

  • In 2006, nearly 23% of Mexican-born workers employed in the United States were in construction: three times the share among the U.S.-born. [1]
